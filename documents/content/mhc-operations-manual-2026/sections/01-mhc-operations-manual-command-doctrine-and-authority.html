1. Purpose

This chapter defines the official command model for MHC operations. It sets authority lines, delegated authority, and succession rules so mission execution is consistent across office and field operations.

2. Command Model (Navy Analog)

  • Commanding Officer (CO): CEO
  • Executive Officer (XO): COO
  • Master Chief: Chief Superintendent (Reagan)
  • CHENG: Safety Officer/CMO (hybrid technical and command bridge)
  • CPCO: Chief Preconstruction Officer (Estimator Officer; acting role currently Todd)

Command intent:

  • The CO sets strategic intent and final authority.
  • The XO executes company-wide operations and controls officer-level functions.
  • The Master Chief leads field execution standards and mission delivery discipline.
  • The CHENG coordinates safety, systems, and cross-functional controls and may escalate directly to CO when required.
  • The CPCO leads estimating command and preconstruction controls and may escalate directly to CO when required.

3. Officer and Execution Bands

Officer band under XO:

  • HR
  • CPCO (Estimator Officer)
  • Estimators (reporting through CPCO as the team scales)
  • CFO (or acting CFO function under COO while role is vacant)
  • PMs and PEs are formally in the XO chain of command.

Execution band under Master Chief:

  • Superintendents
  • Foremen
  • Field crew
  • PMs and PEs directly support Master Chief execution objectives while retaining formal XO reporting.

4. Formal Reporting vs Operational Support

Formal reporting (authority and accountability):

  • PM -> XO
  • PE -> XO
  • Superintendent -> Master Chief -> XO

Operational support (daily mission execution):

  • PM/PE collaborate directly with Master Chief and field leadership for schedule, quality, safety, and closeout outcomes.

5. Succession Rule

If a role is unfilled, command responsibility defaults to the next higher command level until the position is formally filled and authority is transferred.

Current explicit application:

  • CFO role is currently unfilled. CFO command responsibilities are carried by XO.

6. Escalation Rights

Standard escalation path:

  • Field or office issue -> PM/PE or Superintendent -> XO -> CO

CHENG exception authority:

  • CHENG may escalate directly to CO when safety, legal, compliance, or mission-critical technical risk requires direct command visibility.

CPCO exception authority:

  • CPCO may escalate directly to CO when strategic bid exposure, legal exposure, or mission-critical preconstruction risk requires direct command visibility.

7. Command Cadence

Mandatory cadence:

  • Weekly operations control meeting every Monday at 9:00 AM.
  • Quarterly compliance and controls audit led by Safety Officer and/or XO.

8. Approval Doctrine

Manual release sequence:

  • Safety Officer/CHENG review
  • HR review
  • XO approval
  • CO final approval

9. Control Notes

  • This manual is internal and operational.
  • Client-facing materials must not include internal approval thresholds unless explicitly authorized.
  • Command language in this chapter must remain synchronized with future org updates.
