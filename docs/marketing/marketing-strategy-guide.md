## Overview

This guide reflects the v2 marketing operating model for MH Construction. It treats marketing as a relationship-first, evidence-backed operations system rather than a loose set of campaign notes.

The operating model is built around five principles:

- **Ground Truth**: public updates stay grounded in actual progress, approved milestones, and verified field conditions.
- **Zero-Gap Accountability**: marketing, operations, and sales stay aligned so public communication does not drift from jobsite reality.
- **Channel Discipline**: content is planned, published, and repurposed through a defined stack rather than ad hoc posting.
- **Handshake Pride**: the narrative should strengthen trust with Client Partners, Trade Partners, and community stakeholders.
- **Project Lifecycle Continuity**: content runs from deployment through closeout and legacy follow-up so the relationship remains active after the ribbon cutting.

Use this guide when planning a project marketing runway, assigning weekly content, reviewing proof standards, or checking public-facing language against the approved MH brand voice.

## Chapter 2: Core Doctrine and Operating Model

Marketing at MH Construction is not simply posting photos. It is a marketing operations system designed to support lead generation, protect stakeholder trust, and carry the project story from preconstruction through handoff.

The operating model is grounded in the four-value professional foundation system:

- **Honesty**: communicate what is true, current, and approved.
- **Integrity**: do not manipulate outcomes, timelines, or completion status.
- **Professionalism**: present the project team, trade coordination, and client experience with discipline and respect.
- **Thoroughness**: capture the full project story from ground break through closeout and legacy content.

These values build toward the MH end state: trust. Brand language should remain consistent with the approved MH primary slogan: “Built on Quality, Backed by Trust.” Supporting copy should favor clear scope, accountable delivery, dependable follow-through, and relationship continuity over hype.

## Chapter 3: The Marketing Operations Stack

MHC uses a multi-channel media distribution stack so every project update has a clear job: build visibility, reinforce proof, or deepen trusted relationships.

The core stack includes:

- **Radio Ads and Commercials** for market awareness and top-of-funnel brand recognition.
- **YouTube** for long-form proof assets, walkthroughs, interviews, and recap content.
- **Website** as the central hub for case studies, project storytelling, and SEO-aligned trust content.
- **LinkedIn** for B2B authority, owner-facing updates, and professional proof.
- **Facebook** for community visibility, culture, and Trade Partner recognition.
- **Google Business Profile** for local search visibility, short-form trust updates, service-area-facing proof, and review-response discipline using the approved GBP guide and templates.
- **Instagram and X** for visual proof, short updates, and milestone amplification when approved.
- **Email Blasts** for direct communication with stakeholders, referral networks, and long-tail follow-up.

Each channel should publish only what it can support well. The goal is not volume. The goal is clear, evidence-backed communication that reinforces MH as a trusted construction partner.

## Chapter 4: Three Content Pillars

Every piece of marketing collateral should align with one of three pillars so the message stays consistent across channels and project phases.

### Pillar 1: Service-Earned Discipline

Focus on the people, field standards, and disciplined delivery culture behind the work. Use service-earned language that reflects professionalism, safety, and field credibility without sounding promotional.

### Pillar 2: Force Multiplier

Show the technology, integration, and operational efficiency behind the work. This includes spatial capture, digital twins, site documentation, and the systems that support clear coordination, clear handoffs, and zero-gap accountability.

### Pillar 3: Handshake Pride

Highlight the stakeholder network and relationship quality behind the build. This includes Client Partners, architects, city officials, Trade Partners, veterans, and other community stakeholders who define the project story and contribute to long-term trust.

## Chapter 5: The Four-Phase Project Lifecycle Campaign

Each phase uses a plain-language construction description, a branded slogan line, and a proof standard tied to how MH wants the work to be seen in the field and remembered after handoff. The canonical phase names, week ranges, and slogan lines live in [Lifecycle Campaign Parameters](./parameters/lifecycle-campaign-parameters.md).

### Phase I: Deployment

#### Weeks 1-8: Ground Break and Earthwork

**Break Ground, NOT the Schedule.**

Record site prep, initial trenching, and foundation pours. The PM and Superintendent clear drone flights and safe site access so the early record stays accurate, approved, and usable across channels.

### Phase II: Active Tempo

#### Weeks 9-24: Vertical Build and MEP Rough-In

**Raise the Pace, NOT the Pressure.**

Document structural framing, Trade Partner execution, and the active safety posture on site. Align camera crews with major material deliveries and structural milestones so public updates match the most visible progress points.

### Phase III: Closing

#### Weeks 25-32: Finishes and Ribbon Cutting

**Cut the Ribbon, NOT the Cord.**

Capture architectural details, final walkthroughs, and stakeholder handoffs. The PM clears final staging areas to ensure the closeout asset set is clean, professional, and ready for long-tail case-study use while reinforcing that the relationship continues after project completion.

### Phase IV: Legacy

#### Weeks 33-36: Project Debrief and Podcast

**Finish the Project, NOT the Partnership.**

Execute executive interviews, review cost variances, and publish case studies. The CEO and command team schedule studio recording sessions so the completed project produces durable thought-leadership and referral material after handoff.

## Chapter 6: Content Standards and Proof Rules

These standards protect message quality across every project phase and channel. This chapter operationalizes the shared parameter baselines from [Social Media Branding Parameters](./parameters/social-media-branding-parameters.md) and [Proof and Approval Parameters](./parameters/proof-and-approval-parameters.md) inside the broader marketing operating model. If a post, recap, or case-study element does not meet these baselines, it should be revised or held.

### Section 6.1: Channel Baseline

- Primary channels: LinkedIn and Facebook.
- Secondary channels: YouTube, Google Business Profile, Instagram, X, reviews, newsletters, and website case-study surfaces when approved.
- Keep visual identity, naming, and trust framing consistent across channels.
- Match the channel to the message: stakeholder proof on LinkedIn, community-facing visibility on Facebook, local trust visibility on Google Business Profile, and deeper evergreen assets on the website and YouTube.
- Use the Google Business Profile guide when the work involves review cadence, profile upkeep, Q&A management, or local search execution beyond the post itself.
- Use GBP Post Templates when the work calls for local short-form post execution inside the broader campaign plan.

### Section 6.2: Cadence Baseline

- Default pace: one to two posts per week.
- Prefer one proof-driven update and one supporting technical or trust post when the project has enough approved material.
- Reduce cadence when approvals, privacy, or project sensitivity require a slower release rhythm.
- Increase cadence only when verified milestones, media quality, and owner sensitivity all support it.

### Section 6.3: Copy Baseline

- Lead with construction delivery language first.
- Keep veteran field standards as credibility support, not as hype.
- Use plain-language, evidence-backed statements.
- Avoid speculative completion claims, internal assumptions, and unverified performance language.
- Prefer wording that reinforces clear scope, accountable delivery, dependable follow-through, and mission-ready handoff.

### Section 6.4: Proof Baseline

- Only publish updates tied to actual milestones and approved facts.
- Prefer completed-scope photos, approved milestone notes, verified outcomes, and stakeholder-approved summaries.
- Treat privacy, controlled access, and active-jobsite boundaries as mandatory.
- When in doubt, choose the lower-risk proof set and wait for better approved evidence.

## Chapter 7: Guardrails

- Keep language relationship-first, construction-first, and factual.
- Preserve trust and accreditation signals where relevant.
- Use the approved MH primary slogan when a slogan is required, and keep supporting lines aligned with it.
- Do not claim outcomes that cannot be supported by evidence.
- Apply public-sector sensitivity controls when the project touches government or grant-funded work.
- Align page titles, section labels, and descriptors with canonical MH naming rules.
- Avoid internal jargon, hype phrasing, and militarized language that reduces clarity for owner and community audiences.

## Chapter 8: Success Measures

Track the strategy at four levels so performance is measured on consistency, proof, and relationship value, not just output volume:

| Measure          | What Good Looks Like                                                           |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cadence          | Weekly posts are steady and aligned to the active project phase                |
| Proof quality    | Every public post is backed by verified project evidence                       |
| Brand congruency | Voice, naming, and trust signals stay aligned across channels and assets       |
| Conversion value | Content produces calls, reviews, case-study material, or measurable confidence |

## Chapter 9: Deployment and Review

Before publishing a new project marketing run, confirm the following so the campaign stays aligned with MH brand standards and field reality:

1. The project has an approved milestone list and media capture plan.
2. The weekly cadence matches the current delivery phase and sensitivity level.
3. All public copy is evidence-backed and relationship-first.
4. The primary slogan, trust language, and naming stay aligned with MH brand standards.
5. The guide output is regenerated after material changes to sections, labels, or strategy sources.
